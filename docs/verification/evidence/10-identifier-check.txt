  Call           POST /dpp/v1/dpps with the operator's signed token

  Under scheme B the economic operator mints the passport identifier itself. The
  service holds no key for it and cannot alter the DID document afterwards; what
  is wrong at creation stays wrong. The check therefore runs before anything is
  created: does the identifier resolve, and does its serviceEndpoint name the
  host that will actually serve the passport.

  Only the host is compared, never the path. The path carries the ProductID;
  what is under examination is where a reader is sent.

  Three cases, measured against the running system.


1 — The identifier resolves nowhere

  ProductID                 https://dpp.oydapp.eu/01/09520123456791/21/000199

      400 ClientErrorBadRequest

  No passport is created. A passport under an identifier nobody can resolve
  would be unreachable through the discovery path EN 18220 describes.

4 — Cleaned up

      DELETE /dpp/v1/dpps/{DIDOK}   204
      GET    /dpp/v1/dpps/{DIDOK}   404

  The DID itself stays resolvable: the service cannot revoke what it holds no
  key for. That is scheme B, not an omission -- revocation is the holder's
  business.


What this establishes

  Without case 3 nothing would be shown: a check that refused every identifier
  would pass cases 1 and 2 just as well. Only the combination establishes that a
  distinction is being made -- and made on the property that matters.
