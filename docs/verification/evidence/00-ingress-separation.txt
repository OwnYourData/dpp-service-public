Proof of ingress separation — 2026-08-22, after rolling out controller B

Setup
  Controller A   ingress-nginx        IngressClass nginx     externalIPs 89.58.20.114, 152.53.94.38, 152.53.95.183
  Controller B   ingress-nginx-b      IngressClass nginx-b   externalIPs 152.53.34.232
  152.53.34.232 was taken from A and given to B; no DNS name points at that address.

Measured from the operator's machine, outside the cluster

  curl -k https://152.53.34.232/                                        -> 404
      Controller B is alive and knows no host.

  curl --resolve dpp.go-data.at:443:152.53.34.232 https://dpp.go-data.at/
                                                                        -> 404, tls=20
      The same hostname, addressed to controller B: no rule, no certificate.
      tls=20 = unable to get local issuer certificate, so B's default
      certificate rather than A's Let's Encrypt certificate.

  curl https://dpp.go-data.at/                                          -> 200, tls=0
      Unchanged over controller A.

Conclusion
  The two controllers have separate configurations. A hostname is served only by
  the controller whose IngressClass claims it. What decides which custodian
  answers is therefore the A record in the economic operator's zone -- not an
  ingress rule of ours. That is the precondition for measuring the change of
  custodian as an act of the operator.
